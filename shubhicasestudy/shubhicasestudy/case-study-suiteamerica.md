# Revitalizing Hospitality for SuiteAmerica

**Role:** Product Designer (sole designer on this project, working with one Program Manager)
**Company/Client:** SuiteAmerica
**Sector:** Hospitality
**Completion Date:** April 2021

---

## Background

SuiteAmerica, a leader in temporary housing and destination services for over 30 years, wanted to modernize their guest experience with a digital solution. I led the design of a guest portal app that would streamline the entire stay journey — from booking confirmation through check-out — as the sole designer on the project, working alongside my Program Manager. This case study walks through my design process, the challenges I navigated, and the outcomes of building a user-centric mobile application for SuiteAmerica's diverse clientele.

## Core Problem

While SuiteAmerica excelled at providing comfort and convenience during a stay, guests ran into friction around three recurring pain points:

- **Limited access to local information** — guests had no easy way to discover nearby restaurants, parks, or attractions.
- **Inefficient communication** — booking changes and service requests (housekeeping, maintenance) required manual back-and-forth, causing delays.
- **A fragmented experience** — there was no single place for guests to manage their stay, browse local recommendations, or reach SuiteAmerica's services.

My goal: design a guest portal app that unified the entire stay process while also helping guests explore their surroundings — all in one place.

## Solution Overview

I designed a comprehensive guest portal app for SuiteAmerica that addressed the core needs of booking and service management, while also integrating a local search feature that let guests explore nearby restaurants, parks, and attractions directly within the app. The result was a seamless, all-in-one experience that improved convenience and guest satisfaction.

**Key Features:**
1. **Comprehensive Booking Management** — view current, past, and upcoming bookings with detailed information.
2. **Service Request System** — submit and track requests for housekeeping, maintenance, and other services.
3. **Local Search Integration** — discover nearby restaurants, parks, attractions, and more using location-based search.
4. **Personalized Onboarding** — a tailored welcome process to familiarize users with the app's features.
5. **Local Recommendations** — curated suggestions for dining, entertainment, and points of interest.

## Design Process

| Step | What I did |
|---|---|
| **Identify the problem** | I identified the problems preventing guests from completing key tasks and blocking business goals. |
| **Review the current UI/UX** | I audited the app's existing UI/UX to pinpoint what was obstructing users. |
| **Research & analyze** *(with my Program Manager)* | We interviewed 20 recent guests and ran a competitive analysis of the market to understand where existing hospitality apps fell short. |
| **Ideate** | I brainstormed how to improve the UI/UX based on insights from research. |
| **Wireframing** | I wireframed the app to establish content structure and layout. |
| **Hi-Fi Design** | I built high-fidelity designs from the wireframes for client approval and developer handoff. |

The app's structure was organized around four user workflows:
- **Bookings** — centralized access to all booking details.
- **Services** — easy submission of service requests.
- **Local Search** — a dedicated section for discovering nearby areas of interest.
- **Profile & Support** — managing personal details and getting help when needed.

### Research

My Program Manager and I ran interviews with 20 recent guests to understand what "extended stay" actually meant for their day-to-day, alongside a competitive analysis that confirmed no hospitality app in the space had solved local discovery well. From those interviews, I identified three distinct traveler types with very different needs:

- **Business travelers** — wanted speed and reliability: nearest coffee, gym, dry cleaner.
- **Relocating professionals** — wanted to build a mental map of a new city, fast.
- **Families on extended stays** — wanted kid-friendly, ongoing discovery rather than a one-time list.

I used these personas to shape which search filters and result types actually mattered — this is why local search ended up filter-driven rather than a flat directory.

### Design Decisions

I designed the local search experience end-to-end: an autocomplete-driven search bar, category filters (dining, shopping, parks, attractions), and an interactive map view showing results by distance. I made the call to pull in third-party rating data (Yelp/Google Places–style) rather than build a review system from scratch — guests needed to trust the recommendations immediately, and an in-house review system would have taken too long to build credibility.

I validated the design across three rounds: early wireframes with 10 users to sanity-check the structure, high-fidelity Figma prototypes with 15 users focused on task completion, and a beta with 50 real guests before full launch.

## Teachings and Impacts

Post-launch, the app saw a 92% user satisfaction rate and a 45% increase in service request submissions. While local search was one of several features shipped, it was the one guests and stakeholders repeatedly cited as the differentiator versus SuiteAmerica's prior offering — and it cut 30% of routine customer service calls that previously required a human to answer "what's nearby."

If I extended this further, I'd want to move from static local recommendations to ones that adapt as a guest's stay progresses — a business traveler in week one has different needs than the same traveler in week six.
